# Two-Orin 32B Capacity Frontier

Date: 2026-07-30

This report records the tested capacity boundary after stage-local KV-cache,
physical prefill micro-batch, and output-row allocation changes.

## Question

What is the largest Qwen2.5-Coder model that two Jetson Orin Nano 8 GB nodes
can serve with useful context and sustained concurrent requests, and what
latency and throughput does that capacity cost?

## Environment

- two Jetson Orin Nano 8 GB developer kits in `MAXN_SUPER`;
- wired 1 GbE through the same router;
- [Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct Q2_K GGUF](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct-GGUF/blob/main/qwen2.5-coder-32b-instruct-q2_k.gguf);
- artifact SHA-256 `f52ef0a00588ef72ee23b8a40cb7a77143efad9786ac47a27ee318ded1a58f91`;
- llama.cpp revision `bf2c86ddc0685f580595954056c2e77ebabfab4f`;
- 64 transformer layers split 33/31;
- 1,024-token context, F16 activation transport, Q8_0 KV cache, and a
  128-token physical prefill micro-batch;
- greedy sampling and 128 requested output tokens;
- two warmup requests before each six-request serving suite;
- identical node and runtime-worker binaries on both hosts.

For this capacity run, Docker/containerd, firmware-update, modem, camera,
iperf3, and per-user audio services were stopped with runtime-only masks.
Those masks reset on reboot and are benchmark preparation, not JetsonFabric
requirements. Cold host usage before model load remained below 550 MiB per
node.

The 1,024-token context is sufficient for this HumanEval configuration: the
largest rendered prompt is 434 tokens, leaving room for EvalPlus's unchanged
512-token generation cap.

## Capacity

| Node | Layers | Resident tensors | Resident weights | Q8_0 KV per context |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Dopey | 0-32 | 397 | 6,140,276,736 B | 70.12 MiB |
| Grumpy | 33-63 | 374 | 6,166,843,392 B | 65.88 MiB |

The two stages hold 12,307,120,128 resident weight bytes (11.462 GiB) from a
12,313,098,432-byte artifact. The weight payload alone cannot fit in one
8 GB node.

Stage-local Q8_0 KV reduced the per-context cache from 132 MiB to 70.12 MiB on
Dopey and from 124 MiB to 65.88 MiB on Grumpy, a 46.9% reduction on both
stages.

Q8_0 KV alone did not make worst-case concurrent prefill reliable. Two
HumanEval prompts created a 353-token logical batch on each stage; with the
physical micro-batch equal to 353, Grumpy attempted a 211.66 MiB CUDA compute
buffer and failed allocation. Keeping the logical batch at 353 while bounding
llama.cpp's physical micro-batch at 128 reduced that buffer to 76.75 MiB, a
63.7% reduction. Reserving one output row on the final stage, which samples
only the final prompt position, reduced Grumpy's buffer again to 48.33 MiB.
That is 77.2% below the failing allocation. Dopey's intermediate stage also
used a 48.33 MiB compute buffer while retaining every prompt activation. The
same two HumanEval tasks then completed concurrently with no CUDA allocation
or context creation failures.

This remains a thin capacity margin. After the concurrent reproduction,
the full HumanEval run peaked at 43 MiB of swap on Dopey and 80 MiB on Grumpy.
The two allocation controls make this workload viable; they do not create
production-grade memory headroom.

## Serving Performance

| Suite | Success | Output tok/s | TTFT p50 | ITL p50 | E2E p50 | Mean power | J/token |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Pipeline C1 | 6/6 | 2.403 | 1.19 s | 409 ms | 53.32 s | 22.72 W | 9.45 |
| Pipeline C2 | 6/6 | **5.149** | 1.16 s | 377 ms | 49.70 s | 38.15 W | 7.41 |

The output-row and bounded-prefill changes preserved throughput. Relative to
the pre-fix Q8_0 run, C1 throughput was unchanged within rounding and C2 rose
0.2%. Median E2E fell 1.3% at C1 and 1.4% at C2.

A separate 20-request fixed-64-token C1 run measured 2.386 output tok/s,
967 ms median TTFT, 427 ms median ITL, 27.39 s median E2E, 22.66 W mean
cluster power, and 9.50 J/output token.

## Accuracy

| Model | Quantization | HumanEval | HumanEval+ | Fixed-64 output tok/s | J/token |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Qwen2.5-Coder 14B | Q4_K_M | 147/164 (89.6%) | 142/164 (86.6%) | 6.08 | 4.48 |
| Qwen2.5-Coder 32B | Q2_K | **149/164 (90.9%)** | 142/164 (86.6%) | 2.39 | 9.50 |

The 32B result used 164 unique greedy samples generated in one sustained
physical run and scored offline with networking disabled. Its sample file
SHA-256 is
`35aac396c855ff75b435c6152f4156e286c6f7cc045c18b3744ee36e99017ba7`.

The two additional base-test passes do not establish an accuracy improvement.
The paired exact McNemar p-value is 0.754 for HumanEval and 1.0 for
HumanEval+. The 32B 95% Wilson intervals are 85.5-94.4% and 80.5-91.0%
respectively. For this workload, heavy Q2 quantization removes the expected
32B quality advantage while fixed-output throughput falls 60.7% and energy per
token rises 112% relative to 14B Q4_K_M.

This makes 14B Q4_K_M the better measured quality/performance point. The 32B
result remains useful because its weights are 2.48 GiB larger than the 14B
resident set and cannot fit on either node.

## Validation

- All 12 measured serving requests and all 164 HumanEval generations completed
  successfully.
- A distributed CUDA smoke test preserved the expected greedy tokens
  `[11, 304, 264, 4268]`, producing `", in a land"`.
- Activation CRCs matched across every stage boundary.
- Each node loaded only its assigned layer range.
- Both nodes reported `MAXN_SUPER`, CUDA-active execution, Q8_0 KV, the same
  128-token physical micro-batch, runtime revision, and llama.cpp revision.
- Evaluation telemetry covered 142.1 minutes of the run. Mean GPU utilization
  was 88.7% on both nodes, maximum utilization was 100%, and maximum GPU
  temperature was 68.8 C on Dopey and 71.4 C on Grumpy.
- The exact concurrent HumanEval task boundary that previously failed
  completed after bounding the physical micro-batch.
- Neither runtime logged a CUDA OOM, context-creation failure, or HTTP 502
  during the sustained run.

## Claim Boundary

This experiment demonstrates sustained two-node serving for the largest
practical dense Qwen2.5-Coder quantization on this hardware. The next official
[32B Q3_K_M quantization](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-Coder-32B-Instruct-GGUF/blob/main/qwen2.5-coder-32b-instruct-q3_k_m.gguf)
is 15.9 GB before OS, CUDA, KV, activation, and compute-buffer memory, so
process cleanup cannot make it viable on two 8 GB nodes.

The experiment does not show a 32B quality/performance win. HumanEval+ tied
14B, while 32B was 2.55x slower in the fixed-output test and consumed 2.12x
the energy per token. Sparse MoE models may offer a different frontier, but
JetsonFabric's partial-residency patch currently supports Llama and
Qwen2-family graphs only.
